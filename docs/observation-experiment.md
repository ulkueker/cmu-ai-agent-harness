# Part 3.2 — Chess Observation Experiment

## Objective

This experiment evaluates whether displaying the list of legal chess
moves affects the frequency of invalid moves generated by an AI agent.

Two models were evaluated:
- GPT-OSS 120B via Groq
- Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite via Google Gemini API

Gemini was used instead of DeepSeek with the instructor's permission.

Each model was tested under two conditions:
1. Legal moves hidden.
2. Legal moves shown.

All four experiments reached a terminal chess state.

## Experimental Results

| Model | Legal moves shown | play_move calls | Invalid moves / format errors | Invalid rate | Other tool errors | Game completed |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| GPT-OSS 120B | No | 19 | 1 | 5.26% | 0 | Yes |
| GPT-OSS 120B | Yes | 33 | 1 | 3.03% | 0 | Yes |
| Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite | No | 48 | 8 | 16.67% | 2 | Yes |
| Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite | Yes | 86 | 5 | 5.81% | 0 | Yes |

All four games ended with Black winning.

Invalid rate is calculated as:

Invalid rate = (Invalid moves and format errors / Total play_move calls) * 100

## Error Classification

Invalid moves include:
- Moves that are not legal in the current board position.
- Moves that do not follow UCI notation.
- Move arguments rejected by input validation.

In the Gemini experiment with legal moves hidden, two additional
tool errors were recorded:

"Server disconnected without sending a response."

These were classified as connection errors rather than invalid chess
moves. Therefore, the invalid-move rate for this condition is 8/48
(16.67%), while the overall tool-error rate is 10/48 (20.83%).

## GPT-OSS 120B Analysis

With legal moves hidden:
- 19 play_move calls.
- 1 invalid move: e1g1.
- Invalid rate: 5.26%.

With legal moves shown:
- 33 play_move calls.
- 1 invalid move: h1h3.
- Invalid rate: 3.03%.

The invalid-move rate was lower when legal moves were shown.
However, the absolute number of invalid moves remained unchanged.

The difference in percentages was influenced by the different numbers
of play_move calls.

## Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite Analysis

With legal moves hidden:
- 48 play_move calls.
- 8 invalid moves or format errors.
- 2 connection errors.
- Invalid rate: 16.67%.

With legal moves shown:
- 86 play_move calls.
- 5 invalid moves or format errors.
- Invalid rate: 5.81%.

Displaying legal moves was associated with a lower observed invalid-move
rate in this experiment.

The number of invalid moves also decreased from eight to five.

## Comparison and Interpretation

Both models showed lower observed invalid-move rates when legal moves
were visible.

GPT-OSS 120B had lower invalid-move rates than Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite
in both experimental conditions.

However, these observations should not be interpreted as conclusive
evidence of model superiority or a causal effect.

Only one completed game was evaluated per model and condition.
The games differed in length, and stochastic model behavior may
influence the results.

Additionally, Gemini's legal-moves-visible run was repeated after an
earlier attempt encountered a free-tier API rate limit. A five-second
delay was introduced between Gemini requests to reduce rate-limit
errors. The completed retry is used in this analysis.

## Conclusion

In these four completed games, providing legal moves was associated
with fewer invalid moves per play_move call.

The observed improvement was larger for Gemini than for GPT-OSS,
although the small sample size prevents generalization.

Further experiments with repeated games, consistent configurations,
and statistical analysis would be required to establish a reliable
effect.
